# Security and privacy

This describes the system **as built today** (Stages 1 to 3, Stage 4 push notifications, Stage 5 social features, Stage 6 calls, Stage 7 businesses, the admin dashboard and the integration catalogue, Stage 8 payments, and the Stage 9 hardening fixes). Update it with every stage. Nothing here amounts to a claim of legal compliance in any jurisdiction; that needs a regional legal assessment.

## Data we hold

| Data | Where | Protection | Retention |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Email, username, display name, bio, status, locale | `users` | Encrypted in transit (TLS, set up at deployment). At rest relies on disk or volume encryption from the database host. | Until deletion, then scrubbed immediately |
| Password | `users.password_hash` | Argon2id hash only | Until deletion or change |
| TOTP seed | `users.mfa_totp_secret_enc` | AES-256-GCM with `DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, bound to the user id | Until MFA is disabled or the account deleted |
| Sessions (device name, user agent, IP, timestamps) | `sessions` | Refresh tokens stored as SHA-256 hashes only | Revoked rows kept for the security history; deleted with the account. A retention job is still to build. |
| One-time tokens | `one_time_tokens` | SHA-256 hashes; 5 min (MFA), 30 min (reset) or 48 h (verify) | Deleted with the account. A cleanup job is still to build. |
| Blocks | `blocks` | | Deleted with either account |
| Reports | `reports` | | Kept for safety review; the reporter is detached on deletion |
| Audit log (action, actor id, IP, time) | `audit_log` | Append-only from the app; holds no secrets | IP removed on deletion; uuid-only records kept |
| Conversations and membership (who talks to whom, when, read and delivered positions, mute and archive) | `conversations`, `conversation_members` | TLS in transit. **Readable by the server.** | Kept while either person has the account. The deleted person shows as "Deleted account" |
| Message text, replies, mentions, reactions, pins, edit and delete times | `messages`, `message_reactions`, `message_hides` | TLS in transit. **Readable by the server; not end-to-end encrypted.** | "Delete for everyone" clears the text immediately and keeps a tombstone (id, sender, times). Account deletion does the same for every message the person sent |
| Photos, videos, voice notes and files | Object storage (`media/<owner>/<random id>`), metadata in `media` | Private bucket. Short-lived presigned URLs (15 min upload, 5 min download), issued after an access check. **Readable by the server and the storage provider.** | Deleted from storage when the last message using the file is deleted, or when the uploader's account is deleted. Uploads never completed still need a cleanup job |
| Presence (online, last seen) and typing | Redis | Not persisted to Postgres. Typing is never stored | Presence expires about 75 s after the last heartbeat; last seen is kept 90 days |
| One-time WebSocket tickets | Redis (SHA-256 of the ticket) | Single use | 60 s |
| Group chat settings and roles; join and leave times as notices in the chat | `conversations`, `conversation_members`, `messages` (kind `system`) | Readable by the server | Kept with the group. A deleted account leaves its groups; ownership passes on |
| Follows and follow requests | `follows` | | Deleted with either account, and both ways on a block |
| Posts, likes, comments and shares | `posts`, `post_media`, `post_likes`, `post_comments` | TLS in transit. **Readable by the server.** Visibility decided per request by SQL functions | Until the author deletes them, or account deletion (which deletes posts, comments and likes and their files). Moderator removals keep the post hidden with the reason, visible to its author |
| Stories and who viewed them | `stories`, `story_views` | Viewer lists are shown only to the author | 24 hours, then deleted with their files by a sweep (every 5 minutes by default) |
| Communities and membership (role, pending, banned) | `communities`, `community_members` | Private communities show only their name and description to outsiders | Until deleted by the owner; memberships go with the account |
| Push registrations (provider, device token or browser subscription URL and keys, last delivery time) | `push_devices` | Tied to one session. Tokens are never returned by the API or included in exports | Deleted when the session ends (within a minute), when the provider reports the token dead, on deactivation, and with the account |
| Notification settings (on/off, preview level, quiet hours and time zone) | `notification_settings` | | Deleted with the account |
| Call records (caller, callee, chat, voice or video, when it rang, was answered, connected and ended, how it ended, which connection each side used) and a notice in the chat | `calls`, `call_lines`, `messages` (kind `system`) | **Call audio and video never reach the server.** They go between the two devices, or through a TURN relay that sees only encrypted packets (DTLS-SRTP). The API issues each user TURN credentials that expire after `TURN_CREDENTIAL_TTL_SECONDS` | Kept as call history while either person has the account; included in the export |
| Businesses (name, handle, description, category, contact email and phone, address, time zone, currency, opening hours) and their staff with roles | `businesses`, `business_staff`, `business_hours` | Contact details are what the business chooses to publish. Staff lists are visible to staff only | Until the owner closes the business. An owner deleting their account hands it to a manager, or it closes |
| Catalogue items and bookable services | `catalogue_items`, `booking_services` | | Archived, not deleted, so past orders still make sense |
| Orders (who ordered what from whom, the price paid, a note, status changes) and bookings (who booked what, when, a note, whether they came) | `orders`, `order_items`, `bookings` | Staff of that business see the customer's username and display name, never their email or phone. Online orders carry a payment status; card details never reach Nexa (see payments below) | Kept as the business's record. When the customer deletes their account, open ones are cancelled and the rest are detached from them (customer and note cleared). Included in the export |
| Payments and refunds (which order, amount, Nexa's fee, status, the Stripe ids for the checkout, payment and refunds, who paid, refund reasons), payout account status, plans and subscriptions, provider webhook events, and the ledger | `payments`, `refunds`, `business_payment_accounts`, `business_billing`, `subscriptions`, `payment_events`, `ledger_transactions`, `ledger_entries` | **No card numbers, bank details or identity documents**: customers type cards on Stripe's page and businesses give their details to Stripe. Staff of a business see its payments; admins see all of them and the ledger | Kept as financial records. When a customer deletes their account the payment stays but is detached from them. Payments are included in the export |
| Integration grants (which partner integration, which scopes, when) | `integration_grants` | Partners receive nothing yet: no partner API exists | Until revoked or account deletion; included in the export |
| Platform staff roles, report handling (who claimed, resolved, what was done and why) and suspensions (when, reason) | `platform_staff`, `reports`, `users.suspended_at` | Every admin action, and every report a staff member opens, is in the audit log | Kept for safety review |
| Notification queue (recipient, device, message id, state, attempts, error) | `push_jobs` | Holds message ids only, never text; the text is read at send time | Finished jobs deleted after 7 days; deleted with the device, message or account |

**End-to-end encryption:** none. Stage 3 messaging is **not** end-to-end encrypted. The server and anyone with database or bucket access can read messages and files. The apps say this, and nothing may describe chats as end-to-end encrypted until ADR 0004 is implemented and verified. TLS and server-side encryption of TOTP seeds are *not* E2EE.

**What the server can see today:** everything listed above, including message content, files, who talks to whom and when, IP addresses, and read and online state (subject to each person's privacy settings for what *other users* see; the server itself always sees it). Platform staff have no general reading tool. The admin dashboard works from user reports: for a reported message it shows that one message (never the rest of the chat), and for a post, comment, story, community, business or integration it shows that item. Opening a report is itself recorded in the audit log. Admins (not moderators) can look up an account's email address, for account recovery and legal requests.

**Calls:** media is encrypted between the two devices with DTLS-SRTP, which WebRTC always uses. This is not verified end-to-end encryption: nobody compares keys, so a malicious signalling server could in principle sit in the middle. With a direct connection each person's device learns the other's IP address; `ICE_TRANSPORT_POLICY=relay` routes every call through TURN to prevent that. See [`calls.md`](calls.md).

**External processors today:** when a managed TURN provider is configured (Twilio or Cloudflare), it relays encrypted call packets and sees both devices' IP addresses and call timing. The SMTP email provider receives recipient addresses and the text of verification and reset emails. The object-storage provider (whatever S3-compatible service is configured) stores uploaded files. When push is configured, Apple (APNs), Google (FCM) and browser vendors' push services receive device tokens and notification payloads: by default the sender's name and the message text, or less if the person chooses a smaller preview. Web Push payloads are encrypted for the browser, so the browser vendor relays only ciphertext; APNs and FCM payloads are protected only in transit. No analytics, crash reporting or advertising SDKs are included.

## Controls in place
- Argon2id passwords, with timing equalised for unknown accounts.
- Short-lived access tokens checked against a live session on every request, and rotating refresh tokens with reuse detection.
- Rate limits per IP and per account on sign-up, login, two-step, password reset, verification resend and reports.
- Optional TOTP with replay protection.
- Generic credential errors, and password reset that does not reveal which accounts exist.
- Profile lookups return 404 (not 403) when hidden by privacy or a block, so a hidden account cannot be confirmed to exist.
- Web tokens only in `httpOnly` cookies. The API URL stays server-side, and nothing secret is sent to the browser.
- Mobile refresh tokens in the platform keystore. No secrets are compiled into the app.
- Secrets come only from the environment. Weak or missing secrets stop the app from starting, and `.env*` files are git-ignored.
- Logs redact authorization headers, cookies, passwords and tokens.
- Account export, deactivation and deletion are self-service; deactivation and deletion need the password. The export includes messages the person sent and a list of their uploads, and is limited to 5 an hour.
- Re-entering the password in a signed-in session (change password, turn off two-step, deactivate, delete) is limited to 10 tries per 15 minutes per account, and two-step codes to 10 per 15 minutes per account across all sign-in attempts.
- Client addresses for rate limits come only from proxies listed in `TRUST_PROXY` (the API) and from the web server's own proxies (`WEB_PROXY_HOPS`), so a client can't choose its own address.
- The web app sends a Content Security Policy with a fresh nonce on every page (only its own scripts run, no inline handlers, no framing) and HSTS in production.
- At most 20 open WebSockets per person per API instance.
- Messaging permissions: only members can read or write a conversation, and outsiders get 404. Blocks stop messages, reactions, pins and typing in both directions. "Who can message me" (`contacts` or `nobody`) refuses chats from strangers until the person replies.
- Read receipts and online status are shared only when both people allow it.
- Social visibility: posts, comments, likes, stories, community posts and their photos all pass through the same SQL rules (`can_view_post`, `can_view_story`, `can_view_community`, `blocked_either_way`), so a list, a single read and a file download cannot disagree. Audiences (everyone, followers, only me), private accounts and private communities are enforced on the server, and anything hidden is a 404. Sharing a post never widens who can see the original. Blocks remove follows in both directions and hide all content and reactions between the two people.
- Groups: new members cannot read history from before they joined. "Who can add me to groups" and blocks are checked when anyone is added. Admin rights follow a strict hierarchy.
- Moderation: community moderators can remove posts and comments (with a reason recorded in the audit log), approve, ban and remove members ranked below them. Reports can be filed on people, messages, posts, comments, stories and communities. Platform staff work the report queue in the admin dashboard (see below).
- Spam: per-person rate limits on posting, commenting, liking, following, stories, joining and creating communities and groups.
- Uploads: the type is chosen from an allow-list (no SVG, HTML or executables), with size limits per kind. The signed upload URL fixes the content type and length, and the stored file's signature is checked against its declared type before it can be sent. Object keys never contain user input.
- WebSockets authenticate with single-use 60-second tickets, never access tokens in URLs. Browser origins are checked, and sessions are re-checked while the socket is open. There are frame-size and frame-rate limits.
- The web chat reaches the API only through a server-side proxy limited to the messaging, notification, group, social, business and payment endpoints it uses, and the proxy refuses cross-site writes.
- Admin dashboard: only people with a platform staff role, two-step verification on, and a session opened with a two-step code in the last 12 hours (`ADMIN_SESSION_MAX_HOURS`). Turning two-step on in an existing session, or a session older than that, is not enough. Moderators handle reports and suspend accounts; only admins manage businesses, integrations, moderators and the audit log, and only the command line (`admin:grant`) makes someone an admin. Staff accounts can't be suspended through the dashboard, and nobody can suspend themselves. A suspended account is signed out everywhere, its push devices removed, its content hidden like a deactivated account's, and sign-in is refused (only after the right password, so suspension can't be probed). Every action is in the audit log.
- Businesses: staff permissions come from one role table (`apps/api/src/business/roles.ts`) checked on every back-office request; people who aren't staff get 404. Nobody joins a business's staff without accepting an invitation. Order prices are taken from the catalogue on the server, stock is taken in the same transaction, and orders and bookings are idempotent on the client's id. Bookings lock the service while counting places, so a slot can't be overbooked.
- Payments: card details only go to Stripe's hosted pages, so the card-data scope stays at SAQ A. Webhooks are accepted only with a valid `Stripe-Signature` inside the tolerance window, from the right mode, and each event is applied once. Every provider call has an idempotency key. Amounts always come from our own order rows, never from the client. Refunds need `orders.refund` and can't exceed what was paid. Live keys are refused outside production, and the stand-in's API address is refused in staging and production.
- Integrations: no partner code runs in the app and partners get no tokens. A person grants named scopes (never messages, contacts or tokens), can revoke them, and an admin's kill switch pauses every grant at once. An integration that asks for more scopes goes back to review.
- Push: registrations are bound to a session and stop the moment it ends. Web Push subscription URLs are accepted only on known browser push services, so the API cannot be aimed at internal hosts. Notifications are re-checked at send time against blocks, mutes, deletions and read state. Push credentials come only from the environment, are validated at startup, and test-only endpoint overrides are refused in staging and production.

## Before production (not done yet)
- Secret manager (AWS Secrets Manager, GCP Secret Manager or Vault) and a key-rotation procedure for `JWT_ACCESS_SECRET` and `DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY`.
- Database encryption at rest, encrypted backups, restore drills.
- WAF and bot protection on sign-up. (The breached-password check is built: new passwords are checked against Have I Been Pwned by k-anonymity.)
- Privacy policy, terms, cookie notice, records of processing, DPIA, and data processing agreements with providers (**legal**).
- **Before media sharing is opened to the public:** known-CSAM hash matching (for example PhotoDNA, or an equivalent from a child-safety partner), malware scanning of uploads, and NCMEC/IWF reporting procedures. The moderation queue for reports now exists (Stage 7), but it shows reported photos by count only; staff can't view reported media in the dashboard yet. Media sharing works today but **must not launch publicly without these** (**legal and trust & safety**). Profile photos count as public media: anyone signed in who isn't blocked can see them.
- Age gating and a child-safety programme (**legal and trust & safety**).
- Bucket-level encryption at rest and lifecycle rules (expire incomplete uploads) on the production bucket.
- Independent penetration test, against staging (Stage 9 was an internal review: [`hardening.md`](hardening.md)).
- Business verification checks (who is behind a business) before the "Verified" badge means anything to customers; today an admin sets it by hand (**trust & safety**).
- Payments: a real Stripe account with Connect approval, terms for sellers and buyers, refund policy, consumer-law review in each launch country, and Stripe's own data processing terms (**legal**). Dispute handling isn't built.
- Terms for businesses and a partner agreement and security review for each integration before the first goes live (**legal**).
